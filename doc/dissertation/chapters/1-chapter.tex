\chapter{Introduction}

Currently, several areas of technology require software that must execute in real time. This often includes autonomous vehicles, aviation, robotics, industrial systems, and other critical systems where the software is directly responsible for system safety and control. In these domains, a failure to meet timing constraints during execution can lead to severe consequences. Thus, to ensure predictable execution and meet these timing requirements, \gls{rtos} are utilized. These systems are commonly employed in embedded scenarios, where other variables must also be considered, such as energy consumption \cite{rahim2025survey}. When timing and energy consumption constraints are combined with the current widespread adoption of multicore systems, the algorithms responsible for scheduling and load balancing face a considerable challenge \cite{ul2018task, hoffmann2021online}. This scenario motivates the need for predictive algorithms that can capture the state of the system and estimate relevant characteristics of each task in order to schedule and balance them accordingly.

% Problems
One of the major challenges introduced by multicore processors is interference, which arises from task contention for shared resources such as main memory, caches, and I/O peripherals. This contention introduces non-deterministic delays into task execution, making it difficult to accurately estimate the \gls{wcet}. Such interference affects the scheduler's ability to guarantee that tasks will meet their deadlines, thus compromising system reliability \cite{gracioli2019designing, horstmann2019framework, aceituno2023optimized, lugo2022survey}. Heterogeneous multi-core processors introduce additional challenges as well, since they present cores with different characteristics, demanding more sophisticated schedulers and closer attention from load balancing mechanisms. The dynamism and variation of tasks' workloads are another challenge, as tasks may evolve over time, changing their resource utilization and demands and requiring adaptive schedulers \cite{mrabet2024scheduling, jadon2024comprehensive}.

In recent years, several works have proposed the use of \glspl{pmc} in predictive algorithms, relying on hardware information to guide decisions. Most of these approaches rely on \gls{ml} techniques, however, they also report difficulties and pitfalls that hinder their adoption. \gls{ml} solutions usually introduce prohibitive overhead and fail to generalize well to new task-sets and platforms \cite{rahim2025survey}. Moreover, the use of \glspl{pmc} makes it difficult to design architecture-agnostic predictive algorithms. Although some works propose solutions to this issue, they remain limited by problems such as the divergence of \gls{pmc} semantics across architectures, lack of counters in some platform implementations and also noise introduced by the \gls{os} and other external sources \cite{das2019sok}.

Considering this scenario, this dissertation addresses the main problems associated with the use of \glspl{pmc} in \gls{ml} algorithms, offering an analysis based on data collected from a \gls{rtos} running on RISC-V and ARM platforms. We also propose an architecture-agnostic solution that overcomes the identified problems and evaluate its performance using several real-time task-sets.
